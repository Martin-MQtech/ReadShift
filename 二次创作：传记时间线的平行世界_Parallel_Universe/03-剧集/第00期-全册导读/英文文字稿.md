## Movement One · Leaving

[Main narrator]

The story begins with a war.

In 1931, a boy was born into an age of conflict in China. His childhood was not made of grand narratives, but of moving house, again and again.

The bombing of Guangzhou, the fall of Hong Kong, the wartime capital of Chongqing — the first thing a child learns is how to pack his bags.

[Main narrator, slower]

Then came a school desk in Shanghai, and a novel called The Wind Soughs. The boy wanted to be a writer, until his father pressed the dream down with one sentence: "Writing will make you starve."

The era — through the runaway inflation of the gold yuan — rewrote his career for him.

[Main narrator]

In 1949, at eighteen, he boarded a ship for America. He thought it was just another "temporary departure."

He did not know that this departure would become a road of no return.

A year at Harvard, three years at MIT — and so a boy of the East walked into the classrooms of the West.

---

## Movement Two · The Struggle

[Main narrator]

The second movement belongs to semiconductors.

After failing the doctoral exam twice, he sent out dozens of job applications and heard back from almost no one. But fate, through a difference of one dollar, pushed him into a company called Sylvania — and from that day on, he never left chips.

[Main narrator, slower]

In 1958, he joined Texas Instruments. That very year, in that company's labs, Jack Kilby had just lit up the first integrated circuit in human history.

Over the next twenty-five years, he rose from fixing yields all the way to running the largest semiconductor business in the world. He invented "learning-curve pricing," and he made Texas Instruments' integrated-circuit business number one on earth.

[Main narrator]

And yet fate chose to let him lose elsewhere, just when he was at his proudest.

He won the market, but he lost the boardroom. From across half of America, he watched the Traitorous Eight's rebellion in Silicon Valley — not knowing that their script would one day become his own.

[Main narrator, slower]

The hardest battles are not fought in the market, but in the boardroom.

And it was in this very defeat that he learned the most important things he would later carry with him.

---

## Movement Three · Returning

[Main narrator]

The third movement is a journey home.

In 1983, he resigned from Texas Instruments, the company he had served for twenty-five years. At fifty-four, he crossed the ocean again and returned to Taiwan.

[Main narrator, slower]

Sometimes, leaving the wrong position is the most important move in life.

In 1987, at fifty-six, he founded a company that nobody believed in — TSMC.

This company designed no chips. It only made other people's chips. The whole industry was waiting to see it fail.

[Main narrator]

And yet it was this very idea of "the foundry" that let chip designers, for the first time, live without a factory — and that let an island known only for plastic toys and cheap running shoes climb, for the first time, to the summit of world technology.

The disruptor does not play the hero. He builds the platform — and lets everyone else become the hero.

[Main narrator, slower]

In 1997, the Asian financial crisis swept across the region. And it was in the middle of that very storm that TSMC listed on the New York Stock Exchange.

When the storm comes, it is the solid company that gets seen.

## Epilogue

[Main narrator, quiet, slower]

From 1931 to 1998 — sixty-seven years.

One man crossed between civilizations twice in his life: in his youth, from East to West; past fifty, from West back to East.

And in neither crossing did he return empty-handed.

[Main narrator, with feeling]

Now, put on your headphones, or settle into a chair, and open this volume.

Ten episodes. Twenty worlds. Laid out in parallel before you.

Let us walk together into one man's fate — and into the parallel world of an era.

[Main narrator, quiet]

The story of our first volume begins in 1931.

And the legend they call the "sacred mountain that shields the nation" — that legend has only just begun to show its outline.
